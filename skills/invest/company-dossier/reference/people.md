# People, and what the plan pays for

Names and photographs are not the information. **What the compensation plan pays management to maximise** is, because it is the best available predictor of what they will do — and it is disclosed, in full, once a year.

Source: **DEF 14A** (the proxy). Not in XBRL; fetch the document from the filing history.

## What to pull

| Item | Where in the proxy | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Named executives, roles, tenure | Executive officers section | Tenure is the number people skip. A CEO in year two and a CEO in year twelve are different situations |
| **Metrics the annual bonus pays on** | Compensation Discussion & Analysis | This is the section. Revenue, EPS, adjusted EBITDA, free cash flow, a segment target, a safety metric |
| **Metrics the long-term plan pays on** | CD&A, performance share units | Usually relative TSR or a multi-year cumulative measure. Different from the annual plan, and often pulling a different direction |
| Weightings and the payout curve | CD&A tables | Where threshold, target and maximum sit. A maximum reachable at 105% of target is not the same instrument as one needing 140% |
| Mix — salary against equity | Summary compensation table | |
| Ownership | Beneficial ownership table | Shares held outright, separately from unvested awards. They behave differently |
| Insider transactions | Form 4 filings | Distinguish open-market purchases from vesting and from 10b5-1 sales, which are scheduled and carry little signal |

## The one question

> **If the plan pays out at maximum, what did management have to do?**

Answer that in a sentence and most of what follows about their behaviour is already implied.

Adjusted EBITDA with acquisitions excluded from the comparison pays for acquiring. Revenue growth without a return constraint pays for buying revenue. EPS with no share-count adjustment pays for buybacks whether or not they were the best use of the cash. None of these is misconduct; they are instructions, and they are followed.

## Say it, do not judge it

Write what the plan pays for and what it does not measure. **Do not conclude that management is misaligned** — that is a judgement about people, from a document that has been drafted by lawyers to be defensible, and this file does not make judgements.

The useful version is: *the annual plan is 60% adjusted operating income and 40% revenue; neither has a capital-return constraint; the long-term plan is three-year relative TSR against a 14-company peer group listed on page 44.* A reader can do their own arithmetic from that.

## The board

Size, average tenure, independent proportion, whether the chair and CEO are the same person, and the audit committee's financial expert. Facts, one line each. Long average tenure is not a defect and short is not a virtue — record it and move on.

## What is not here

Anything from an interview, a profile or a conference appearance. **The dossier reads filings.** Once impressions of people enter, the file stops being something whose every line can be traced to a document, and the discipline that makes the rest of it worth reading is gone.
